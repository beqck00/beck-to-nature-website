> What works:
> 
> * The hero is the best part. The dotted world map with gold arcs flying toward America explains the business in one picture, and the large serif headline looks expensive and calm.
> * “One booth. One week. A clear answer.” is excellent copy, and the short paragraph next to it is clear.
> * The inquiry is well built: “Question 1 of 6”, four clear choices, a progress bar and one button. That’s the frictionless flow you wanted.
> * It uses one accent colour, gold, on near-black, and the serif with italic accent words feels consistent and premium.
> 
> What holds it back:
> 
> * The hand-drawn gold line that snakes down the page is the biggest problem. It runs straight through the inquiry card, the founder quote and both case stories, so you’re reading text with a line across it. The “Beck” signature also appears four times, which turns a nice touch into a gimmick. I’d keep it once, in the hero or the footer, and drop the line.
> * The middle “Medical & healthcare” card has a gold marble texture behind small text. It’s hard to read, and it’s the element that looks most AI-generated. A flat card with a thin gold border would show which one is featured without the noise.
> * The glowing metallic gold buttons look dated, closer to a casino or crypto site than Apple. A flat gold or white pill would look more serious.
> * The text is too small and too faint. Most body copy is small grey on black, which tires the eyes, and older buyers at hospitals and distributors will struggle with it. Body text should be 18 px or more, with lighter grey.
> * The italic last word (“specialize.”, “inquiry.”, “founder.”, “stories.”) repeats in every heading, so it starts to feel like a template. Use it once or twice.
> * The layout keeps switching between left-aligned, centred and split, and there are very large empty gaps between sections, so the page feels long and loose.
> * It doesn’t fit the brand. The Beck To Nature logo is lime green and brown, and this page has none of it. Black and gold also suggests luxury, law or finance more than a reliable trade-show partner for a CT/MRI maker. A dark site can work, but for medical buyers a light page usually feels more trustworthy.
> 
> In short, the atmosphere is 8/10, readability 5/10 and trust for this audience 6/10. If you remove the snaking line, flatten the buttons and the featured card, and enlarge the text, it gets to about 8.

> 4 steps to turn a plain settings screen into a clear one 👇
> 
> 1. Sections. Nine rows in one list are hard to scan. Group them under titles.

> Sorry the text contrast is bad 😭 btw some design law definitions:
> 
> - Squash & stretch: things squish on impact and stretch in motion, which gives them weight
> - Anticipation wind-up: a small pull back before the big move, so people see it coming
> - Staging the focus: direct the eye to one thing at a time
> - Pose to pose: set the key poses and let the browser fill in the frames between
> - Follow through: parts keep moving after the main body stops, then settle
> - Slow in & slow out: speed up and slow down, never move at a constant speed
> - Moving in arcs: move along curves, not straight lines
> - Secondary action: a small flourish that backs up the main action
> - Snappy timing: duration sets the feel, so keep UI animations under 300ms
> - Exaggerated feedback: push it past real so the moment lands
> - Solid drawing in 3D: give flat things depth and volume
> - Overall appeal: the sum of every detail, done with care
> - Fitts’s law: bigger, closer targets are faster to hit
> - Bigger hit areas: the clickable area can be bigger than it looks
> - Hick’s law: more choices mean slower decisions
> - Miller’s law: we hold about 7 things in mind at once, so chunk information
> - Doherty threshold: respond within 400ms or it feels slow
> - Postel’s law: accept messy input, give back clean output
> - Jakob’s law: people expect your app to work like the apps they already use
> - Frequency of use: the more often something is used, the less it should animate
>

> Follow these animation rules in every UI you build:
> - Entrances: start from scale 0.97 with opacity 0, never from scale 0
> - Buttons: scale to 0.97 while pressed, with button:active { scale: 0.97 } and transition: scale 150ms ease-out
> - Wrong passwords: shake the field (translateX 0, -10, 10, -6, 6, -3, 0px over 360ms) as well as turning it red
> - Steppers: slide the next step in from the direction you’re going instead of crossfading
> - Accordions: :root { interpolate-size: allow-keywords }, so height: auto animates smoothly
> - Moving things: animate transform, never top or left, so the browser doesn’t repaint every frame
